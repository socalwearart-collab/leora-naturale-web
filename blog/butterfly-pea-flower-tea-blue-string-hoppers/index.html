Butterfly Pea Flower: Blue Tea and Blue String Hoppers

Nil katarolu turns water a vivid blue. Here is how to brew butterfly pea flower tea and use it to colour string hoppers, rice and desserts naturally.

What is butterfly pea flower?

Butterfly pea (Clitoria ternatea) is a climbing vine with deep blue flowers. In Sinhala it is නිල් කටරොළු (nil katarolu) and in Tamil சங்குப்பூ (sangu poo). Dried, the flowers brew into a caffeine-free drink with a vivid, natural blue colour. Leora Naturale packs 100% dried butterfly pea flowers in Sri Lanka, 10 g per pouch.

How do you make butterfly pea flower tea?

  1. Put a few dried flowers in a cup.
  2. Pour over warm water.
  3. Steep for 3 to 5 minutes, until the water turns deep blue.
  4. Strain and enjoy it plain, or with honey.

Why does it turn purple with lime?

The blue colour reacts to acid. Squeeze in a little lime and the cup turns violet in seconds, which children love to watch.

How do you make blue string hoppers?

Brew a strong cup of butterfly pea tea and let it cool. Use it instead of plain water when you mix your idiyappam flour, then press and steam the string hoppers as usual. They come out a soft natural blue with no artificial colour.

What else can I colour with it?

  • Rice: add the brew to the cooking water for blue rice.
  • Desserts: colour puddings, jelly and icing.
  • Drinks: make blue lemonade, then watch it change with lime.

How should I store it?

Keep the pouch sealed in a cool, dry place so the flowers stay crisp and the colour stays bright.
